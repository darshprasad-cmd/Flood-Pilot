# Languages

दिशाAI speaks the seven languages Delhi actually speaks.

| Language | Script | Why it is here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| हिन्दी Hindi | Devanagari | Dominant official language |
| English | Latin | Business and media standard |
| ਪੰਜਾਬੀ Punjabi | Gurmukhi | Large cultural influence |
| اردو Urdu | Nastaliq (RTL) | Strong historical presence |
| বাংলা Bengali | Bengali | Key neighbourhood pockets |
| भोजपुरी Bhojpuri | Devanagari | Growing migrant community |
| मैथिली Maithili | Devanagari | Significant regional dialect |

This is not a generic i18n starter set. Delhi's flood risk falls hardest on
exactly the populations least likely to be reading English — the migrant
communities in the trans-Yamuna colonies and the low-lying settlements along the
floodplain. A flood warning that only exists in English is a warning that misses
the people who most need it.

Bhojpuri and Maithili are written as genuine languages rather than
Devanagari-transliterated Hindi: **बा** not है, **रउआ** not आप, **काहे** not
क्यों for Bhojpuri; **अछि**, **अहाँ**, **किएक**, **कतय** for Maithili.

---

## What is translated, and what is not

**Translated.** The entire interface, plus every safety-critical string: risk
levels, confidence bands, decision actions, vehicle survivability verdicts,
route comparison labels, timeline headings, report types, the onboarding
conversation, the Today dashboard, and the operations dashboard. 292 keys
across 23 namespaces, at full parity in all seven locales.

**The brand is not translated.** दिशाAI stays in Devanagari in every locale,
including Gurmukhi, Bengali and Nastaliq, because it is a name rather than a
word. `landing.meaningNote` explains what दिशा means *in* the reader's
language, around the Devanagari word itself.

**Not yet translated.** The engine's generated explanations — the sentences that
interpolate live numbers, such as *"The Najafgarh Drain runs 70 m from this road
and is 62% silted against its design section."* These are composed at runtime in
`src/lib/engine/explain.ts` from the model's own feature contributions, so
translating them means translating templates rather than strings.

This is a real limitation and the UI states it: the language picker carries a
note, and `meta.translationNote` is present in every dictionary.

**Not reviewed by native speakers.** Key parity, placeholder integrity and the
absence of untranslated English have all been verified mechanically. Whether
`30 सेमी — ना चले लायक` reads to a Bhojpuri speaker in the Yamuna Pushta the way
the engine means it has not been. That needs a human reader per locale, and
until it happens this is the most important open item in the project.

## How it works

```
src/lib/i18n/
  locales.ts          Locale registry: code, native name, direction, script, role, font
  index.tsx           Provider, useT() and useI18n()
  messages/
    en.ts             Source of truth — every other locale is typed against it
    hi.ts pa.ts ur.ts bn.ts bho.ts mai.ts
```

`Messages` is derived from the English object with literal types widened back to
`string`, so **adding a key is a compile error in every locale until it is
translated**. That is deliberate: a half-translated flood warning is worse than
an untranslated one, because the reader cannot tell which half they are missing.

### Client-side, not routed

Localisation is client-side (`localStorage` + `<html lang dir>`) rather than
routed (`/hi/app`). The trade-off, stated plainly: every existing route and API
path keeps working unchanged, at the cost of the first server-rendered HTML
always being English. For an application people open, choose a language in, and
then use — rather than a page they land on from search — that is the right way
round. Moving to routed locales later means adding a `[locale]` segment; nothing
in the dictionaries changes.

### Right-to-left

Urdu sets `dir="rtl"` on the document. Two things are deliberately *not*
mirrored:

- **The map.** Geography does not flip. `.leaflet-container` is forced back to
  `direction: ltr` so Leaflet's pane transforms, controls and tooltips stay
  correct.
- **Numbers.** `.numeric` spans are isolated with `unicode-bidi: embed` so a
  depth of `31 cm` does not reorder inside an RTL sentence.

Layout uses logical properties (`ms-`, `me-`, `start-`, `end-`,
`border-inline-start`) where it matters. RTL polish is good, not perfect —
worth a pass by a native reader before launch.

## Adding a language

1. Add an entry to `LOCALES` in `locales.ts` with its native name, direction,
   script, font stack and the reason it is being added.
2. Copy `messages/en.ts` to `messages/<code>.ts`, type it `Messages`, translate.
3. Register it in `DICTIONARIES` and `LOCALE_ORDER` in `index.tsx`.
4. Add the font to `FONT_HREF` in `src/app/layout.tsx` if it needs a new script.

TypeScript will tell you if you missed a key.

## Before you ship this

**These translations have not been reviewed by native speakers.** They were
produced with care and follow the terminology Delhi actually uses — जलभराव for
waterlogging, नाला for a trunk drain, अंडरपास borrowed as-is — but this is
safety-critical content telling people whether to drive into water.

Get a native reader on each language before a public launch, and Bhojpuri and
Maithili especially, where the line between the language and Hindi is easy to
blur.
